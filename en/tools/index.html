Theta accelerates as expiry approaches: an at-the-money option loses value fastest in its final weeks. Shorten T and watch the curve collapse onto the payoff.

How it is computed
  • C = S·N(d₁) − K·e^(−rT)·N(d₂); P = K·e^(−rT)·N(−d₂) − S·N(−d₁)
  • d₁ = [ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)·T] / (σ·√T); d₂ = d₁ − σ·√T
  • N(x) is computed through erf (Abramowitz–Stegun 7.1.26, error below 1.5·10⁻⁷).
  • Δ = N(d₁) for a call, N(d₁) − 1 for a put; Γ = φ(d₁)/(S·σ·√T); ν = S·φ(d₁)·√T / 100 (per 1 pp of σ); Θ per day = Θ per year / 365.
  • Assumptions: European exercise, no dividends, constant σ and r, continuous compounding.

Same drift and volatility, plus only rare downward jumps (one every two years on average, typically around −14%). The median barely moves, but the 5% VaR and the expected shortfall grow substantially. The normal model would call such a drop practically impossible; the paths below its VaR level are drawn in oxide red.

How it is computed
  • Sₜ₊Δₜ = Sₜ · exp[(μ − σ²/2)·Δt + σ·√Δt · z], Δt = 1/252 (daily step), z ~ N(0, 1) via Box–Muller.
  • Jump model (Merton 1976): with probability λ·Δt per day a jump exp(J) arrives, J ~ N(−0.15, 0.10²), λ = 0.5 per year; the drift is compensated by λ·κ·Δt (κ = e^(μJ + σJ²/2) − 1) so the expected price stays the same.
  • 5% VaR = S₀ − 5th percentile of terminal values; 5% ES = S₀ − mean of the worst 5% of terminal values; P(loss) = share of paths with Sₜ < S₀.
  • Both models are always computed from the same seed so the columns can be compared; the chart shows the selected model.
  • Note: Student-t innovations on daily steps average out over longer horizons (CLT) and the terminal distribution approaches the normal one, which is why crashes are modelled as jumps.

The model is sensitive to its inputs: shifting expected returns by 1–2 percentage points reshuffles the "optimal" portfolio substantially. That is why raw Markowitz is rarely used on its own in practice; constraints, shrinkage estimators or Black–Litterman views are layered on top.

How it is computed
  • E[rp] = Σ wᵢ·μᵢ; σp² = Σᵢ Σⱼ wᵢ·wⱼ·ρᵢⱼ·σᵢ·σⱼ; Sharpe = (E[rp] − rf) / σp; Σ wᵢ = 1, without shorting wᵢ ≥ 0.
  • Frontier = upper envelope of 3,000 random portfolios (no shorting: weights from normalised |N(0,1)|; with shorting: from N(0,1) limited to [−1, 2]) plus the two-asset mixes.
  • Estimation error: 20 scenarios with μᵢ ± 1.5 pp and σᵢ ± 10% (fixed seed); their frontiers are drawn and the half-range of the max-Sharpe weights is reported as ±.
  • The three correlations entered must form a positive definite matrix; I require its smallest eigenvalue to be at least 0.05. If it is not, all three are scaled towards zero by the same factor until it is, and a note appears under the chart.
  • Assumptions: one period, no transaction costs; the inputs are themselves estimates.
